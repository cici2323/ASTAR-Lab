Research

What we build, and why it has to work outdoors.

Four connected areas, from robots that share a task with a crew to robots that inspect what nobody should have to climb. Every project is measured against real site conditions: clutter, weather, bad light, and a schedule that does not wait.

R1

Human–robot collaboration

We build humanoids and mobile manipulators that work alongside a crew instead of replacing it. The current focus is a vision-based perception-and-action framework: the robot watches a skilled worker, recovers the structure of the motion, and reproduces it on the real task.

On top of that sits the interface. Large language models let someone assign and correct work in plain language, and earlier work in the group used brainwave signals for hands-free control when a worker's hands were already busy.

R2

Autonomous field robotics

A job site is cluttered, half-built and rarely mapped, which is exactly where most mobile robots stop working. We develop perception, planning and control that survive those conditions, and we validate in simulation before taking hardware outside.

The flagship platform is aerial-legged: it takes off like a drone, lands on a structure, then switches to legged locomotion to cover the last few metres where flight is unsafe or too imprecise.

R3

Worker safety and sensing

Prevention is cheaper than response. Computer vision detects fall hazards and flags postures that will cause injury over a season rather than in one moment. Wearable physiological sensing estimates fatigue and cognitive load in real time.

The point is not surveillance. It is giving a robot enough awareness of the person beside it to slow down, take over the heavy part, or ask before acting.

R4

Infrastructure inspection

Inspecting roofs, facades and elevated steel puts people on ladders and ropes for hours. We fuse vision, depth and other modalities with 3D reconstruction so a robot can cover the structure instead, including the parts that are dark, occluded or awkward to reach.

The output matters as much as the autonomy: a reconstruction and a defect record that an engineer can check, compare against last year, and sign.
